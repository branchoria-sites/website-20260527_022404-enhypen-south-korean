## Introduction

The four songs that best explain ENHYPEN are “[Given-Taken]({{ 'given-taken/' | relative_url }})”, “[Drunk-Dazed]({{ 'drunk-dazed/' | relative_url }})”, “FEVER” and “Bite Me”. Together, they trace the group’s central dramatic movement: uncertainty becomes exhilaration, exhilaration becomes desire, and desire becomes a dangerous form of connection. They also show why ENHYPEN’s vampire imagery works. Blood, thirst, curses and surrender are not merely decorations; they give physical shape to anxieties about debuting, being watched, wanting someone and depending on them.

<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Enhypen_the_South_Ko_177f17_essential_songs_dc7642-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Essential Songs" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Each track captures a different strength. “Given-Taken” turns the group’s survival-show origin into an unusually self-questioning debut. “Drunk-Dazed” makes sudden fame sound like a party spinning out of control. “[FEVER]({{ 'fever/' | relative_url }})” slows that chaos into intimate longing. “[Bite Me]({{ 'bite-me/' | relative_url }})” then compresses ENHYPEN’s ideas about fate, sacrifice and performance into one of their clearest pop statements. Heard in sequence, the songs reveal a group whose appeal lies in making vulnerable emotions feel theatrical without making them feel unreal.

## Why “Given-Taken” remains the defining starting point

“Given-Taken” is the essential first chapter because its title asks a question that only ENHYPEN could have asked so convincingly at debut: was their opportunity given to them, or had they taken it through competition and effort? The [members]({{ 'members/' | relative_url }}) had just emerged from *I-LAND*, where places in the group were determined through evaluations and public voting. BELIFT LAB’s description of *Border: Day One* stresses both their happiness at surviving and their anxiety about crossing from trainee life into an unknown future. In an interview with the Recording Academy, ENHYPEN called “Given-Taken” a song suited specifically to their situation at that moment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://beliftlab.com/artist/discography/ENHYPEN/1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beliftlab.com">[BELIFT LAB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beliftlab.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The debut album is comprised of six tracks overarching various topics that anyone can relate to: “Intro: Walk the Line”, lead single “Gi...</span></span></span>

That ambiguity gives the song more weight than a conventional arrival anthem. It does not present debut as a clean victory. Instead, the group sound as though they have entered a new world without knowing whether they belong there. The restrained verses, recurring harp figure and dark electronic pulse create suspense rather than uncomplicated triumph. Even when the chorus expands, its melody retains a searching quality. The result is a debut song built around doubt: success has happened, but its emotional meaning remains unresolved.

The supernatural imagery strengthens that tension. Fangs, blood and transformation suggest that crossing into idol life has changed the members permanently. Becoming visible brings power, but it also threatens innocence and control. That duality would become one of ENHYPEN’s most reusable ideas: an apparently desirable transformation always carries a cost.

“Given-Taken” also established their performance grammar. The [choreography]({{ 'choreography/' | relative_url }}) relies on shifting formations, controlled gestures and sudden changes of level rather than constant brute force. The members frequently appear linked, separated and rearranged, making the group itself look unstable before it settles into alignment. The precision matters because ENHYPEN’s songs often describe loss of control while their bodies display extraordinary control. That contradiction — emotional uncertainty expressed through exact movement — is fundamental to their stage identity.

Not every critic regarded the track as a fully formed debut statement. Some reviews found its opening slow or its vocal processing too heavy. Yet those reservations help explain why it remains revealing: “Given-Taken” is less an instant crowd-pleaser than a blueprint. Its atmosphere, moral uncertainty and polished unease provided material ENHYPEN would sharpen on later releases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thebiaslist.com/2020/11/30/song-review-enhypen-given-taken/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thebiaslist.com">[Bias List]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thebiaslist.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bias List Song Review: ENHYPEN – Given-Taken</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bias ListSong Review: ENHYPEN – Given-TakenNovember 30, 2020 — 30 Nov 2020 — The song takes way too long to get going, wasting its first...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 30, 2020</span></span></span>

## How “Drunk-Dazed” turns fame into a carnival

Where “Given-Taken” hesitates at the doorway, “Drunk-Dazed” rushes through it. BELIFT LAB framed *Border: Carnival* as the story of the members entering a glittering new world after debut, where ordinary rules no longer seem to apply. ENHYPEN themselves described the period as exciting but hectic: everything appeared glamorous, unfamiliar and overwhelming, like a carnival.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://beliftlab.com/artist/discography/ENHYPEN/2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beliftlab.com">[BELIFT LAB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beliftlab.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">BELIFT LABENHYPEN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2nd Mini Album BORDER: CARNIVAL“Drunk-Dazed” amidst a magnificent carnival, ENHYPEN expresses their sincerest emotions facing the n...</span></span></span>

The song translates that experience directly into sound. Its pounding rhythm, distorted textures and repeated hook barely allow the listener to recover between sections. Rather than describing intoxication from a safe distance, the production creates it: the track surges forward as though the party has become impossible to leave. Rock-edged percussion adds abrasion to the dance-pop framework, giving the celebration a threatening undertow. Contemporary reviews repeatedly noted its relentless energy and its success in making the album’s carnival concept audible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thebiaslist.com/2021/04/26/song-review-enhypen-drunk-dazed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thebiaslist.com">[Bias List]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thebiaslist.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">song review enhypen drunk dazed</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bias ListSong Review: ENHYPEN – Drunk-Dazed26 Apr 2021 — I like how Drunk-Dazed kicks off at high energy and doesn&#x27;t let up. This is why “Drunk-Dazed” is more than a high-energy comeback. Its excitement and fear are inseparable. Sudden attention is thrilling, but it produces disorientation; being the centre of the spectacle feels powerful, but the spectacle can consume its participants. Vogue characterised the song as a reflection of ENHYPEN’s post-debut rollercoaster, while the members discussed exhaustion, demanding schedules and the need to support one another through unfamiliar pressures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vogue.com/article/meet-enhypen-your-latest-k-pop-obsession" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vogue.com">[Vogue]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vogue.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Meet ENHYPEN, Your Latest K-Pop Obsession</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meet ENHYPEN, Your Latest K-Pop Obsession</span></span></span>

The music video makes the metaphor deliberately excessive. A grand party becomes a blood-soaked nightmare, with fountains, chandeliers and formal styling collapsing into supernatural chaos. Blood functions simultaneously as drink, inheritance and danger. The members appear to belong at the celebration and to be trapped inside it. That visual uncertainty mirrors the song’s perspective: they are intoxicated by the new world even as they recognise that it may be changing them.

On stage, “Drunk-Dazed” demonstrates ENHYPEN’s ability to turn group synchronisation into narrative. The choreography is rapid and densely organised, with sharp head movements, collapses and rebounds that suggest bodies being pulled by forces outside themselves. Yet the formations remain disciplined. The dancers look dazed without the performance ever becoming messy. This combination of apparent abandon and hidden exactness is one reason the track became such a durable concert centrepiece.

## Why “FEVER” changed the temperature

“FEVER” explains the other half of ENHYPEN’s appeal. It is not as explosive as “Drunk-Dazed”, but its slower pace reveals how effectively the group can sustain tension without relying on spectacle. Teen Vogue summarised the song as being about desire experienced like an illness, and that idea is present in every part of the arrangement: the subdued beat, breathy delivery and recurring melodic phrases make longing feel physically inescapable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.teenvogue.com/story/enhypen-take-us-inside-their-camera-roll-to-celebrate-new-ep-border-carnival" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teenvogue.com">[Teen Vogue]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teenvogue.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on teenvogue.com.</span></span></span>

Placed on the same EP as “Drunk-Dazed”, “FEVER” feels like the private aftermath of the public carnival. The earlier song is overwhelmed by lights, noise and attention; “FEVER” reduces the world to the body and the person it desires. Heat replaces intoxication as the governing image. The emotional danger is no longer that the members might lose themselves in a crowd, but that they might willingly give themselves to someone else.

The choreography is crucial to the song’s impact. Rather than matching every beat with aggressive movement, it uses controlled body lines, floor work and repeated gestures around the neck, wrist and torso. Those details connect sensuality to the group’s vampiric imagery without requiring an elaborate narrative explanation. During ENHYPEN’s *Manifesto* tour, the song appeared in a red-toned section shaped by blood and moon imagery; the members discussed costume choices designed to support gestures such as biting fabric during the choreography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.teenvogue.com/story/enhypen-manifesto-world-tour-nyc-style-interview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teenvogue.com">[Teen Vogue]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teenvogue.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Teen Vogue ENHYPEN Take Us Backstage at &quot;Manifesto&quot; World Tour in New York City</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teen Vogue ENHYPEN Take Us Backstage at &quot;Manifesto&quot; World Tour in New York City</span></span></span>

“FEVER” also broadened the group’s audience beyond listeners drawn primarily to title tracks. Its success showed that an ENHYPEN B-side could become a signature song through mood, performance and replay value. It offered a less plot-heavy entry point into the group: a listener did not need to understand the wider fantasy narrative to recognise the song’s depiction of consuming attraction.

Its restraint distinguishes it within the catalogue. Many ENHYPEN songs build drama through large contrasts, forceful refrains or abrupt switches. “FEVER” instead holds the listener inside one sustained emotional state. The track’s menace comes from patience. Desire does not explode; it lingers, weakens resistance and gradually takes control.

Together, “Drunk-Dazed” and “FEVER” form an especially useful pair. The first externalises pressure as a chaotic celebration, while the second internalises it as bodily longing. One is crowded and dizzying; the other is close and claustrophobic. Both describe surrender, but at different scales.

## What “Bite Me” clarifies about connection

“Bite Me” is the clearest later expression of ENHYPEN’s central themes because it turns the vampire bite into a request rather than an attack. The speaker asks to be marked by another person, presenting connection as proof of fate and as an act of surrender. BELIFT LAB’s account of *Dark Blood* describes blood as the link between “you” and “I” and frames the song around a character prepared to sacrifice himself to protect a destined partner.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://beliftlab.com/artist/discography/ENHYPEN/6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beliftlab.com">[BELIFT LAB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beliftlab.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on beliftlab.com.</span></span></span>

That shift matters. Earlier songs often portray transformation as something imposed on the members: they cross a border, enter a carnival or become overwhelmed by desire. In “Bite Me”, the central figure chooses the bond. The danger remains, but agency has changed. Connection is frightening because it may erase independence, yet separation is presented as worse.

Musically, the song is strikingly economical. It draws on early-2000s boy-band pop, using clipped rhythmic phrases, a direct refrain and spacious production rather than the maximal impact of “Drunk-Dazed”. NME’s review of *Dark Blood* praised its refrain and its revival of that earlier pop vocabulary, while other critics admired its drama but argued that its brevity and lack of a conventional bridge prevented the arrangement from reaching a fuller climax.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nme.com/news/music/enhypen-dark-blood-mini-album-3434466" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nme.com">[NME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nme.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">enhypen dark blood mini album 3434466</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">enhypen dark blood mini album 3434466</span></span></span>

That structural debate is revealing. “Bite Me” can feel intentionally unresolved: the song ends before desire has been safely contained. Its compactness makes it instantly memorable, but it also denies the listener the release that a larger final chorus might provide. Whether heard as elegant restraint or missed escalation, the abrupt ending reinforces the sense of a bond still being negotiated.

The performance makes the song’s relational theme visible. Pair choreography with female dancers turns the bite into a duet, replacing the largely self-contained masculine formations common in idol staging with mirrored movements, approaches and withdrawals. Ni-Ki contributed to the choreography, and the paired staging became one of the era’s most discussed elements. Some fans organised protest trucks demanding its removal, an episode reported internationally and widely criticised as an example of possessive fan expectations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.teenvogue.com/story/enhypen-members-talk-sacrifices-songwriting-favorite-karaoke-songs-interview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teenvogue.com">[Teen Vogue]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teenvogue.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on teenvogue.com.</span></span></span>

The controversy should not overshadow the artistic choice. The choreography gives physical meaning to a song about mutual dependence: another body is not background decoration but the force that completes the movement. It also makes “Bite Me” an important performance marker for ENHYPEN, demonstrating greater confidence in presenting desire as an interaction rather than an abstract pose.

These tracks are not simply four popular points in ENHYPEN’s discography. They form a compact emotional sequence:

* **“FEVER”** turns spectacle inward, making desire feel private, physical and consuming.
* **“Bite Me”** presents surrender as a deliberate choice made in pursuit of an inseparable bond.

The progression also shows how ENHYPEN use fantasy. Their vampire concept is most effective when it translates a recognisable emotional conflict. A bite can mean transformation, intimacy, danger or belonging. Blood can represent appetite, lineage, sacrifice or proof that two people are connected. A carnival can embody fame because it is dazzling, artificial and exhausting at once.

Sound and choreography perform the same work. Dark electronic pop supplies unease; rock and dance textures create momentum; slower rhythmic tracks make intimacy feel tense rather than soft. Meanwhile, exact formations, neck gestures, falls, floor work and partnered movement turn abstract emotions into images that can be understood even without following every narrative clue.

This is the core of ENHYPEN’s appeal: their songs do not ask listeners to choose between personal confession and elaborate concept. At their best, the concept is the confession. “Given-Taken”, “Drunk-Dazed”, “FEVER” and “Bite Me” make anxiety, attention, longing and attachment large enough for an arena while preserving the uncertainty that made those feelings compelling in the first place.
